# REDSHIFT: the story

The plot, the world and the words on screen. Ash Calder is they/them. Colours
are MODE 1's eight, given for the Red Phase; the Blue swaps each for its
opposite (red-blue, yellow-cyan, magenta-green; white stays), as in
`rooms/rooms.txt`.

## 1. The backstory

*For the manual.*

Kharon-4 is the fourth moon of a gas giant nobody got round to naming: cold,
airless and dull, apart from **doublespar**, a clear crystal that splits a beam
of light into two perfect halves. Every jump drive in settled space aims
through a sliver of it, and Kharon-4 is the only place it has ever been found.

So Tallis Deepworks sank a shaft, built a town on top and shipped in nine
hundred miners. The deeper they dug, the purer the spar. Chief Assayer Hedda
Morrell wrote three memos asking why, as it looked to her like something
further down was flaking. The memos were filed under LATER.

At 03:12 on 14 March 2189, Bore Nine found out. The drill cut into a wall that
was not rock: white, faceted, faintly warm and, once touched, awake. Every lamp
in the mine went red, then blue, then both. The forty-one miners of the night
shift at Bore Nine stopped being anywhere a radio could reach.

The Company calls it **the Prism**, because it is faceted and it splits things.
It split the mine into two mines in the same rock, the Red Phase and the Blue,
and something moved into each. In the Red: war machines built from the
Company's own carts, drills and cranes, still stamped with Company serial
numbers and nothing else familiar. In the Blue: a crystal swarm that grows out
of the spar like frost on a window. Nobody knows who built the Prism, or
whether it is a weapon, a factory or a door. Everyone agrees it is on, and the
split is climbing the shaft towards the town.

Morrell ground the last good doublespar on the surface into lenses for eight
**phase harnesses**: armoured suits that let the wearer choose which phase to
stand in, and switch at will. Each harness maps itself to one nervous system
and will not take another. Seven troopers went down in seven harnesses. Seven
stopped answering.

The eighth belongs to **Trooper Ash Calder** of the Kharon Garrison, forty
troopers whose job until March was breaking up fights on payday. Ash is
thirty-one, twice reprimanded for wasting ammunition, which the colony now
regards as a qualification. Ash was on the cage gate the night Bore Nine broke
through, and waved the night shift down. Ash asked for the harness first.
Nobody argued.

Your orders: go down through five sectors, find the heart of the Prism and
switch it off. There is no spar left for a ninth harness.

## 2. The two phases

The Prism splits reality the way a prism splits light, by wavelength.

**The Red Phase** is the long half: hot, heavy and slow. Light comes out
stretched into reds and yellows, the air smells like a foundry, and sound
arrives late. The war machines live here. They don't grow; they build, and what
they build is barricades, welded plate across tunnels that were open yesterday.

**The Blue Phase** is the short half: cold, thin and quick. Light comes out
squeezed into blues and greens, frost furs every rail, and footsteps ring. The
swarm lives here. It doesn't build; it grows, and what it grows is spans,
crystal bridges thrown across pits as if it wanted to get somewhere.

The rules, as Morrell's harness manual gives them:

- **What was there before Bore Nine is in both phases**: rock, girders,
  pipework, acid. What has been made since is in only one: machine plate in the
  Red, swarm crystal in the Blue. So a bridge can hold Ash in one phase and
  drop them in the acid in the other.
- **The visor draws the other phase in dotted outlines.** The lens catches a
  little of the other half's light: enough to warn, not enough to touch.
- **Nothing crosses but light.** A Trundler in the Red can walk straight
  through Ash in the Blue and neither will feel a thing. Bolts and grenades
  stay in the phase they were fired in.
- **When Ash shifts, the whole world recolours**, because Ash is now seeing by
  the other half of the light.
- **Every shift cracks one phase cell**, a sliver of doublespar.
- **The interlock will not shift Ash into anything solid.** Morrell fitted it
  after the trials. Nobody talks about the trials. (It has no opinion about
  shifting Ash into anything hot.)

## 3. The five sectors

### Sector 1: THE LEACH WORKS

*Where the Company dissolved ore out of rock with acid, and left the acid
behind. Pipes, vats, catwalks, and machines still finding their feet.*

- **Look:** rust-red rock over yellow lamps, magenta pipework, white girders;
  acid green in the pits. In the Blue: blue rock, cyan lamps, green pipes,
  magenta acid, as in LANDING ZONE.
- **Rooms:** LANDING ZONE, PUMP HOUSE, VAT ROW, THE PICKLE JAR, SETTLING TANKS,
  FOREMAN'S HUT, THE CATWALKS, SLURRY RUN, SKIP SHAFT.
- **Hazards and enemies:** acid pits, swarm bridges (Blue), the first welded
  barricades (Red), a cracked wall to teach the grenade. Trundlers and Chimes;
  one Drill Sergeant guarding the SKIP SHAFT.

### Sector 2: MARSHALLING YARD

*The ore railway's hub, and the machines' home ground. Every ore cart has grown
legs, every crane has grown a gun, and the timetable is still on the wall.*

- **Look:** yellow hazard-striped steel and rails on magenta rock, red signal
  lamps, white sparks; in the Blue, cyan steel on green rock, blue lamps.
- **Rooms:** ENGINE SHED, SIGNAL BOX, TURNTABLE, WEIGHBRIDGE, SIDING FOUR,
  CRANE GANTRY, BUFFER STOPS, DEAD MAN'S HANDLE, CRUSHER'S MOUTH.
- **Hazards and enemies:** Red barricades everywhere, so going round costs
  cells; torn rail ends as spikes; walls of jammed ore carts for grenades.
  Trundlers in numbers, Drill Sergeants in the signal boxes, a few Chimes.

### Sector 3: THE SUMP

*Every mine leaks, and the leaks all run here. In the Red Phase the sump boils.
In the Blue it is bone dry, because the swarm drank it.*

- **Look:** magenta steam over yellow sodium lamps on red brick, the water red
  and boiling at the bottom. In the Blue: green fog, cyan lamps, blue brick,
  and the water only a dotted outline over a dry bed.
- **Rooms:** OUTFALL, THE CISTERN, BOILER WALK, STEAM TRAP, DROWNED OFFICE,
  SLUICE GATE, THE U-BEND, OVERFLOW, THE PLUGHOLE.
- **Hazards and enemies:** boiling water only in the Red (a hazard tile with
  the red flag), so the dry bed in the Blue is the road while the cells last.
  Shift back in the wrong place and you're in the soup. Chimes and Slivers in
  the Blue, Trundlers on the Red walkways above.

### Sector 4: THE SPAR SEAMS

*The deepest the Company dug, and the reason it dug at all: doublespar in veins
as thick as a man. The swarm grows here, and the light comes out of the walls.*

- **Look:** the Blue is native here: cyan and white crystal on deep blue rock,
  a green glow along the floors. In the Red the seams go yellow and white on
  red over a magenta glow: the inside of a furnace.
- **Rooms:** THE MOTHER LODE, SPAR FACE, THE ASSAY ROOM, SORTING TABLE, THE
  CHANDELIER, SPLIT SEAM, ECHO GALLERY, LAST PAYDIRT, BORE NINE.
- **Hazards and enemies:** crystal spikes, spar walls for grenades, long swarm
  spans over deep drops. Slivers in clouds, Chimes in chords, and Drill
  Sergeants dug in round BORE NINE, guarding something.

### Sector 5: INSIDE THE PRISM

*Past Bore Nine the rock stops. White walls, true angles, a hum you feel in
your fillings, and floors that are only there in one phase at a time.*

- **Look:** the Prism's walls are white in both phases. Around them, thin bands
  step through every colour: red, magenta, yellow in the Red; blue, green, cyan
  in the Blue. Use every band the engine allows.
- **Rooms:** FIRST FACET, THE REFRACTOR, CRITICAL ANGLE, DOUBLE IMAGE, SPECTRUM
  WALK, INTERFERENCE, FOCAL LENGTH, THE DARK FRINGE, THE HINGE.
- **Hazards and enemies:** floors of alternating Red and Blue blocks, so Ash
  shifts to stay standing as much as to fight; white spikes in both phases; few
  cells. Every enemy, both phases at once. Then the Hinge.

## 4. The enemies

The machines are Red Phase; the swarm is Blue.

- `sentry`: **DRILL SERGEANT** (Red). A Company drill head bolted into the
  rock, with a gun where the bit used to be. Blaster bolts spark off its plate;
  a grenade cracks it open. Or shift and walk past, if you can spare the cell.
- `crawler`: **TRUNDLER** (Red). An ore truck that grew six legs and opinions.
  Walks a floor end to end, turns at walls and edges, and does not stop for
  anyone.
- `drone`: **CHIME** (Blue). A hollow crystal the size of a helmet, riding a
  lazy wave through the air and ringing one note, slightly flat. Lovely to look
  at. Lethal to touch.
- `shard`: **SLIVER** (Blue). A finger of crystal that turns to face Ash and
  then comes, slowly, without giving up. The smallest thing in the swarm, and
  the only one that takes an interest in you personally.

The pickups, and the way out:

- `cell`: **PHASE CELL.** A thumb of cut doublespar in a steel sleeve, glowing
  faintly. Racked in Company emergency lockers, and lying wherever the seven
  troopers before Ash dropped them.
- `ammo`: **CHARGE PACK.** Blaster charge, cached by the first rescue teams,
  before rescue teams stopped going down.
- `grenades`: **FRACTURE CHARGES.** Company blasting charges, repacked by the
  Garrison armourer into something a trooper can throw.
- `exit`: **THE CAGE.** A Company cage lift whose cable still holds; step in to
  ride down to the next sector. At BORE NINE it is the drill shaft itself, and
  it goes straight into the Prism.

### The boss: THE HINGE

At the Prism's focus sits the one thing in the mine that is in both phases at
once. In the Red it is a war engine, all plate and guns, a Drill Sergeant the
size of a house. In the Blue it is a crystal flower that sheds Slivers. Both
halves share one core, and what happens to one half happens to the other.

To kill it, grenade the plate in the Red until it cracks, and a matching crack
opens in the crystal. Shift, and blast the core through it before the crystal
grows back. Repeat. The floor is a patchwork of Red and Blue blocks, so every
shift is also a choice of where Ash will be standing, and cells are few.
Morrell's files call it the Hinge because everything turns on it. Pilot, lock
or heart: nobody knows.

## 5. The endings

**Victory.** Ash's last bolt goes into the core. The Hinge folds shut, the
Prism goes dark one facet at a time, and with a sound like a very large door
closing, the two mines slide back into one. The lamps come up white. At Bore
Nine, forty-one miners blink and ask why it has gone so quiet; their shift
ended six weeks ago. Ash walks them to the cage. Morrell gets to mention her
memos. And the visor, which should have nothing left to show, still draws one
thing in dotted lines: a second Prism, deeper down, facing the other way.

**Out of lives.** The harness cracks its last cell and stops choosing. Ash goes
where the night shift went: into neither phase, a dotted outline on nobody's
visor. The split climbs the shaft. Tallis Deepworks records the loss of
Kharon-4 in its quarterly report, under Other.
